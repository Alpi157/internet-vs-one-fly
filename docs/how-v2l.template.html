How it works

A fly brain, simulated from the real wiring diagram of a fruit fly. Here is what is real, what is simplified, and what I found.

From your hand to its jump

  1. Your hand

    Falls toward the fly and grows in its view.

  2. Its eyes

    Looming detectors (LC4, LPLC2) fire when something grows fast.

  3. {{game_n}} simulated neurons

    Connected as mapped in a real male fruit fly. Spikes travel through that wiring.

  4. Escape neuron

    The giant fiber and its partners send the takeoff command.

  5. Jump

    If it fires in time, the fly is gone.

Everything after your hand is simulated. The escape is not scripted: no line of code says “jump when the hand is close”. Walking also runs through neurons: a random push to the walking neurons stands in for the fly’s own urge to explore.

What is real, what is simplified

Real

  • Who connects to whom, and how many contacts, from MaleCNS: a complete map of a male fruit fly’s nervous system.
  • The neuron shapes you see: real skeletons from the same map, simplified for display.
  • Every flash: a spike that actually happened in the simulation.

Simplified

  • Each neuron is a simple spiking unit, not a detailed cell.
  • Vision is two numbers: how big the hand looks and how fast it grows.
  • Whether a connection excites or inhibits is assumed from the cell’s transmitter.
  • The urge to walk is a random push added by the game. The wiring model does not walk on its own.
  • Body, walking and takeoff physics are basic.

The experiment in one minute

I shuffled who connects to whom, keeping each neuron’s number of connections. The shuffled brains exploded into activity while the real one stayed calm.

Then a stricter test, registered in advance: shuffle again, but keep each neuron’s total input. Most of the gap disappeared. In this model, how unevenly input is spread matters more than the exact partners.

Words used here

Connectome
A map of every neuron and every connection in a nervous system.
Spike
A short electrical pulse a neuron sends to its partners.
Looming detectors
LC4 and LPLC2, eye cells that respond to something approaching fast.
Escape neuron
The giant fiber, a large cell that can trigger a takeoff.
Preregistration
Writing down a test and how to judge it before running it, so the result cannot steer the method.
